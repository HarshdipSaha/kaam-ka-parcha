# Public Hindi/Hinglish audio for ASR benchmarking

Research note checked 2026-10-05. Sources below are first-party dataset pages, source repositories, or the original papers/specifications. The private benchmark handoff commits its selected 120-clip CC BY 4.0 IndicContextEval Hindi sample with attribution under `data/benchmarks/real_audio_2026-10/`. User-provided YouTube-source audio, captions, and source identifiers remain local because source-specific redistribution permission is not recorded; corresponding result rows redact transcript/reference text.

## Recommendation

Start with the Hindi split of [IndicContextEval](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ai4bharat/IndicContextEval): it has 3,110 Hindi utterances (9.08 hours, 124 speakers), read and extempore speech, a `code_mixing` flag, and 23 professional domains including **Skilled Trades**. For a workshop-adjacent slice, inspect Hindi rows with `domain_name == "Skilled Trades"`, `speech_style == "Extempore"`, and `code_mixing == true`; listen-review the selected rows before calling them mechanic examples. The domain label does not guarantee that a clip is about vehicle repair. The dataset is labeled CC BY 4.0 and the card gives a direct loading path. [Dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ai4bharat/IndicContextEval) · [AI4Bharat paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19157)

Add two independent stress slices: [Vaani Benchmark V1.0](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ARTPARK-IISc/Vaani-Benchmark-V1.0) for geographically diverse Hindi/code-switching and three-reference scoring, and the [Gram Vaani 3-hour evaluation set](https://www.openslr.org/118/) for spontaneous regional Hindi telephone speech. The latter is limited to academic use unless Gram Vaani grants permission for commercial use. Use [Kathbath-Hard in Vistaar](https://github.com/AI4Bharat/vistaar) as a controlled noise probe, clearly labelled synthetic noise. I found no first-party dataset page that identifies a Hindi mechanic/customer repair-estimate test set; a small, consented workshop recording set is needed to make claims about that exact use case.

## Benchmark design guidance

- Report results separately by source and slice: scripted/clean, spontaneous professional, Hindi-English code-switching, telephony, and added-noise. Vistaar's original work compares multiple sources because source content, speakers, equipment, and collection methods change recognition difficulty. [Vistaar paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15386)
- Fix and publish the transcript-normalization rules, and show at least WER plus CER. Preserve both raw and normalized outputs for Hindi script, Latin-script English words, numerals, and punctuation. Use multiple references where available: Vaani's paper explicitly finds single-reference WER can overstate errors when valid spellings/transcriptions differ. [Vaani paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21408)
- Track named-entity accuracy as well as aggregate WER. IndicContextEval reports a named-entity error rate and varies domain/entity context from a bare prompt through English/native-script entity lists and a wrong-domain control. This is a useful design for measuring whether terminology hints help or cause hallucinated parts. [IndicContextEval card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ai4bharat/IndicContextEval)
- For this job-card product, add task metrics beyond ASR: exact item/repair extraction, critical amount exact match in paise, omitted/extra line items, and correct total. These are project-specific measures inferred from the confirmed-card workflow; a low WER alone does not prove an estimate is safe to seal.
- Sample across unique speakers, regions, speech styles, and recording conditions. Keep a source/speaker-level manifest and record the API/model version, request settings, date, failures, latency, and credit units per clip. Keep raw audio and personal metadata out of commits; publish aggregate results unless redistribution is explicitly permitted.

## YouTube-derived audio

Publicly viewable does not itself mean permission to download, convert, submit to an API, or republish. YouTube's [Terms of Service](https://www.youtube.com/t/terms) restrict downloading or otherwise using content except as permitted by the service, with prior permission from YouTube and applicable rights holders, or as allowed by law. For the already-converted clips, keep the source URL and evidence that the uploader/rightsholder permits the specific benchmark and external API processing. Exclude a clip if those rights or the source's license are unclear. Dataset licenses also do not automatically settle whether a contributor's voice may be sent to a third-party API; check access/consent conditions for each source.
