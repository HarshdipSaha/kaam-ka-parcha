# R5: What makes Indian AI demo posts travel on LinkedIn and X (and what Gnani amplifies)

> Ticket: `docs/wayfinder/tickets/R5.md`. Researched 2026-10-04 (day 4 of the contest; 37 days to the Nov 10 close).
> Methods: WebSearch, WebFetch, Bing/Yahoo via `ddgs`, X's public syndication endpoint (`cdn.syndication.twimg.com/tweet-result`) for like/reply counts on individual posts, logged-out LinkedIn post pages for reaction counts.
> Not used: Playwright, Gnani APIs, Reddit (hard 403 without a browser), xquik (needs OAuth).
> **Caveats:** X like/reply counts below come from the syndication endpoint (no view or repost counts). LinkedIn counts are what the logged-out page shows and age fast. Vendor blog statistics (AuthoredUp, dataslayer, opentweet, gromming, stan.store) are secondary readings of Richard van der Blom's paywalled Algorithm Insights Report and of X's open-sourced code; where sources disagree I say so. Anything marked **[speculation]** is my inference.

## 1. Executive summary

1. **Gnani lives on LinkedIn, not X.** The company page has ~124K followers and its challenge announcement drew 115 reactions / 7 comments in 3 days; the identical post on X (`@GnaniAi`, with video) has **14 likes and 3 replies**, and a June 2025 post has 3 likes ([LinkedIn page](https://www.linkedin.com/company/gnani-ai/), [X post](https://x.com/GnaniAi/status/2105582718222585919)). CEO Ganesh Gopalan (27K LinkedIn followers) out-performs the company page on the same news (174 likes / 10 comments) and "regularly reshares employee and community content" ([profile](https://in.linkedin.com/in/ganesh-gopalan)). **Implication:** the LinkedIn post is the one that can get amplified by Gnani people; the X post is mostly a compliance checkbox, but on X a student account with a good video can out-reach the sponsor itself.
2. **Gnani's own posting style is text-only, enterprise, metric-led** (call-outcome stats, BFSI reports, NeurIPS acceptance, GFF booth). Nothing in their feed resembles a consumer-facing demo video. A student demo that is *visual, human and specific* fills a gap rather than competing with them.
3. **The field is wide open so far.** The first student entry found under the hashtag (Venkata Sai Kowshik Boddu, "IndicVoiceCare", a generic STT→LLM→TTS pipeline with a demo video) sits at **5 reactions, 1 comment** after ~1 day ([post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/venkata-sai-kowshik-boddu_gnaniai-greatindianaiinternshipchallenge-activity-7512093942717509632-0fsm)). Gnani employees reposting the announcement got 1–21 reactions each. The bar for "top engagement" among entrants may be in the low hundreds of reactions **[speculation]**.
4. **2026 platform mechanics favour exactly what a student can do:** LinkedIn now distributes by *interest*, not follower count (van der Blom: "your follower count no longer predicts your reach", 1.3M posts) ([launch post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/richardvanderblom_rvdbcarousel-algorithm-insights-report-2026-activity-7455148601749729280-AwsY)); small accounts have the highest reach *rate* (0–500 followers: top-10% posts reach 367% of follower count, top-1% reach 3,028%) ([Shield Index Feb 2026](https://theshieldindex.beehiiv.com/p/february-2026-linkedin-reach-benchmarks-by-follower-bracket)). X's open-sourced ranker applies a new-author boost and sends ~50% of For You to non-followers ([xai-org/x-algorithm README](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm), [opentweet](https://opentweet.io/blog/x-algorithm-secrets-2026)).
5. **What the algorithms pay for is the same on both platforms: conversation and dwell, not likes.** LinkedIn: comments ≈2× likes, replies-to-comments 2.4×, saves 5×, first 60–90 min decides ~70% of reach, only ~5% of slow starters recover ([AuthoredUp](https://authoredup.com/blog/linkedin-algorithm), [dataslayer](https://www.dataslayer.ai/blog/linkedin-algorithm-february-2026-whats-working-now)). X legacy weights: reply 13.5, author-replies-back 75, bookmark 10, repost 1, like 0.5, report −369 ([opentweet GitHub read](https://opentweet.io/blog/x-algorithm-open-source-github-2026)).
6. **Native video, under 60–90 s, vertical, burned-in captions, link in the first comment (LinkedIn) or in a reply (X), ≤3 hashtags, post at 8–9 AM IST (LinkedIn) or 9–11 AM / 8–9 PM IST (X), and sit on the post for 90 minutes answering every comment.** Details in §3.
7. **What goes viral in India is people, not pipelines.** The highest-engagement Indian AI clips found were a grandmother talking to ChatGPT in Hindi (2M+ views, 78K likes, 1,854 comments), a 17-year-old's ₹25,000 robot teacher saying "Hello baccho, mera naam Sophie hai", a Canadian's POV walk-through of a Bengaluru hacker house (1.4M+ views), and a redemption-arc thread about Sarvam (1,630 likes). Pure product-launch posts from the best-funded Indian AI company get ~1,500 likes on X only because it is Sarvam. See §4.
8. **Implication for idea choice (§7):** pick a demo whose *60-second clip* has (a) a real person speaking a real Indian language/code-mix, (b) a visible before/after or a visible "it understood that?!" moment, (c) a ₹/time jugaad number, and (d) a one-line relatable problem. Score candidates with the rubric in §7.3; anything under 14/21 will need paid-grade luck to clear a few hundred reactions.

## 3. Platform mechanics, 2026

### 3.1 LinkedIn

**Distribution model.** Van der Blom's 2026 report (1.3M posts, 50K creators, 1,400 hours) says LinkedIn moved from a relationship graph to an **interest graph**: ~60% of initial reach goes to interest clusters rather than connections; follower count "no longer predicts reach"; reach for active creators is down ~60% over two years, views −50%, follower growth −59% ([launch carousel](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/richardvanderblom_rvdbcarousel-algorithm-insights-report-2026-activity-7455148601749729280-AwsY), [stan.store summary](https://stan.store/blog/linkedin-algorithm/), [Leoni summary](https://www.leoniconsultinggroup.com/blog/linkedin-algorithm-2026-what-drives-reach-now), [Creator Science ep. 307](https://www.tapesearch.com/episode/307-richard-van-der-blom-the-state-of-linkedin-in-2026-based-on-data-from-1-3-million-posts/NWiEQRMqjA4NEXF6QKKjUq)). Company pages grew 33% while personal profiles fell; **personal profiles still out-engage pages 2.60% vs 1.74%** ([Metricool 2026](https://metricool.com/linkedin-statistics/)). Post from your personal profile.

**Golden hour.** Posts are tested on 2–5% of the network for 60–90 minutes; ~70% of total reach is decided there; only ~5% of under-performers recover; a threshold of roughly 10–20 genuine commenters in hour one is cited ([expandi/socialbee via search](https://socialbee.com/blog/linkedin-algorithm/), [hyperclapper](https://www.hyperclapper.com/blog-posts/linkedin-organic-reach-decline-2026-algorithm-penalty), [dataslayer](https://www.dataslayer.ai/blog/linkedin-algorithm-february-2026-whats-working-now)). Your own replies to comments count as engagement; top-1% creators reply to comments 741% more often than average ([SocialPilot, Sept 2026](https://www.socialpilot.co/blog/linkedin-algorithm)). **Only post when you can stay online for 90 minutes.**

**Engagement weights.**
- Comments weigh ~2× likes in initial classification; replies-to-comments give 2.4× reach vs. standalone; saves ~5× a like (and ~2× a comment per SocialPilot) ([AuthoredUp](https://authoredup.com/blog/linkedin-algorithm), [SocialPilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/blog/linkedin-algorithm)).
- March 2026 "Authenticity Update": comment quality is scored by length (<5 words ≈ zero), relevance, thread depth (replies count ~3× standalone); engagement bait ("comment YES", "tag someone") is penalised; pods detected at "~97% accuracy"; polls collapsed to 0.07% engagement ([gromming](https://gromming.com/blog/linkedin-algorithm-update-march-2026), [SocialPilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/blog/linkedin-algorithm), [dataslayer](https://www.dataslayer.ai/blog/linkedin-algorithm-february-2026-whats-working-now)). This is also what Gnani's rules ban, so the two incentives align.
- Dwell time: a post read for 45 s outperforms one with 200 quick likes; carousels average 55 s dwell (highest); targets cited: 75%+ consumption, 15%+ completion ([gromming](https://gromming.com/blog/linkedin-algorithm-update-march-2026), [stan.store](https://stan.store/blog/linkedin-algorithm/)).

**Formats.**
| Format | 2025 reach multiplier (personal) | 2026 reading |
|---|---|---|
| Document/carousel (PDF) | 1.45× | Still top; 6.6% engagement rate; 8–12 slides; highest dwell |
| Native video | 1.10× | 5.6% ER for 30–90 s; video views +36% YoY; vertical 9:16 currently boosted; under 60–90 s; "no longer a massive boost" but strong for small accounts in the video tab |
| Image + text | 1.18× | 3.2% ER |
| Text | 0.88× | 2.0% ER; 34% of top posts are now <600 chars (21% in 2024); AuthoredUp says 800–1,000 chars optimal |
| Poll | 1.64× (2025) | Dead after March 2026 (0.07%) |
| External link in body | worst | −45% to −60% reach; preview card removal softens it |

Sources: [AuthoredUp](https://authoredup.com/blog/linkedin-algorithm), [dataslayer](https://www.dataslayer.ai/blog/linkedin-algorithm-february-2026-whats-working-now), [rivereditor 300-post test](https://rivereditor.com/blogs/2026-linkedin-algorithm-what-works-300-posts-tested), [visla](https://www.visla.us/blog/guides/linkedin-video-in-2026-whats-working-and-how-to-make-it/), [van der Blom comments](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/richardvanderblom_rvdbcarousel-algorithm-insights-report-2026-activity-7455148601749729280-AwsY).

**Video tab.** LinkedIn's dedicated vertical video feed (launched late 2024) is "where creators with under 5,000 followers can break out"; viewership +36%, video creation +100%; India saw a 60% increase in video uploads vs 45% globally; captions raise watch time 30–40% because most scroll muted ([LinkedIn video 2026 roundups](https://linkedinpreview.com/blog/linkedin-video-strategy-2026), [Inc42 on India rollout](https://inc42.com/buzz/linkedin-looking-to-launch-its-video-offering-in-india), [Lindsey Gamble](https://lindseygamble.com/blog/linkedin-is-testing-a-short-form-video-experience-including-a-new-video-tab)).

**Links.** The "link in first comment" workaround is weakening: gromming measures a 10–15% penalty on it (vs 45–55% in-body), SocialPilot says "patched"/"bridge behaviour detected", van der Blom (via stan.store) says comment links get *hidden* and advises in-body links only when conversion matters more than reach ([gromming](https://gromming.com/blog/linkedin-algorithm-update-march-2026), [SocialPilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/blog/linkedin-algorithm), [stan.store](https://stan.store/blog/linkedin-algorithm/)). **For the nominated post: no link in body; put GitHub/live-demo link in the first comment and in your profile's Featured section.** (A separate, un-nominated post can carry the link.)

**Hashtags.** Hashtag following and hashtag feeds were removed in late 2024; hashtags now only aid search. Data range from "no impact" to −5–10%; 3–5 slightly hurts, 6+ seriously hurts (AuthoredUp); Metricool counter-claims +85% impressions with ≥1 hashtag ([AuthoredUp](https://authoredup.com/blog/linkedin-algorithm), [contentin](https://contentin.io/blog/do-hashtags-work-on-linkedin/), [metricool](https://metricool.com/hashtags-on-linkedin/)). **Use exactly the two required tags, nothing else.** The IndicVoiceCare post used seven.

**Tagging.** Tagging 5+ people where 50%+ don't engage triggers spam detection (van der Blom comment thread); repeated non-engaged tags degrade the account ([van der Blom post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/richardvanderblom_rvdbcarousel-algorithm-insights-report-2026-activity-7455148601749729280-AwsY), [askcruit](https://www.askcruit.com/branding/linkedin/linkedin-tagging-etiquette)). Tag the company page plus ≤2 humans who will actually respond (teammate, a professor, a Gnani engineer you've already talked to on Discord).

**Other penalties.** Posting twice within 24 h cannibalises reach; 3–4 posts/week optimal; editing >5% of the text after publishing; >5 emojis; multiple blank lines (dwell manipulation); copy-pasted AI text (−30% reach, −55% engagement) ([stan.store](https://stan.store/blog/linkedin-algorithm/), [hyperclapper](https://www.hyperclapper.com/blog-posts/linkedin-organic-reach-decline-2026-algorithm-penalty), [SocialPilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/blog/linkedin-algorithm)). Write the post by hand.

**Reposts.** Bare reposts get minimal reach; "repost with your thoughts" performs like an original post; commenting on the original often reaches more people than resharing ([gromming](https://gromming.com/blog/linkedin-repost-algorithm)). When friends want to help, ask them for a *comment with a sentence of substance*, then a repost-with-thoughts, not a bare repost.

**Realistic reach for a student account.** Shield Index, 50K posts, Feb 2026:

| Followers | Typical reach (impressions ÷ followers) | p75 | p90 | p99 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0–500 | 41% (~94 impressions) | 115% | 367% | 3,028% |
| 500–1K | 27% (~199) | 64% (~484) | 174% (~1,332) | 2,470% |
| 1–2.5K | 22% | 52% | 139% | 1,514% |

([Shield Index](https://theshieldindex.beehiiv.com/p/february-2026-linkedin-reach-benchmarks-by-follower-bracket)). So a 700-follower account's *normal* post gets ~200 impressions and maybe 5–15 reactions; a *top-decile* post gets ~1,300 impressions; a breakout gets 15–20K. Engagement rate at this size is high: 2–5% of impressions become reactions **[speculation from typical ratios]**, so 100–300 reactions needs roughly 5–10K impressions, i.e., a p95+ post or external distribution (§6).

**Timing (IST).** MagicPost, 220,220 Indian posts to May 2026: best **8 AM IST weekdays** (score 81/100), strong through 9 AM, flat 62–63 afterwards; top slots Mon 9 AM (100), Mon 8 AM (96), Wed 8 AM (96), Fri 8 AM (88); Indian posting *volume* peaks at 10 AM, so 8 AM beats the crowd; weekends weak (Sun noon best, 79) ([MagicPost India](https://magicpost.in/blog/best-time-to-post-on-linkedin-india)). Other India sources: Tue–Thu 9–11 AM, secondary 1–2 PM and 6–8 PM; Wednesday beats Tuesday in India ([wscubetech](https://www.wscubetech.com/blog/best-time-to-post-on-linkedin/), [reactin](https://www.reactin.io/blog/best-time-to-post-on-linkedin)). **Choose Tue/Wed 8:00–8:30 AM IST and be free until 10:00.**

### 3.2 X

**Open-sourced ranker (Jan 2026, updated May 2026).** `xai-org/x-algorithm`: the Phoenix model predicts per-viewer probabilities of favorite, reply, repost, quote, share, DM-share, profile click, link click, video quality view (VQV), dwell, follow, and negatives (not-interested, mute, block, report); score = Σ weight_i × P(action_i) (`xai-value-model/scoring.rs`, weights in `home-mixer/params/param.rs`); post-ranking applies **author diversity decay**, an **out-of-network discount**, and a **new-author boost** ([README](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm)). Third-party readings add: ~50% of For You is out-of-network via SimClusters; a post loses ~half its score every ~6 h; accounts with TweepCred <65 get only 3 posts/cycle considered; the `lte_1000`-follower bucket faces an extra spam screen ([opentweet](https://opentweet.io/blog/x-algorithm-secrets-2026), [postory](https://postory.io/blog/x-algorithm-2026), [opentweet GitHub read](https://opentweet.io/blog/x-algorithm-open-source-github-2026)).

**Legacy weights still quoted as directionally right:** author replies back to a reply +75; reply +13.5; profile click +12; link click with 2+ min dwell +10–11; bookmark +10; repost +1; like +0.5; video watched to 50% +0.005; mute/block −74; report −369 ([opentweet](https://opentweet.io/blog/x-algorithm-open-source-github-2026)). **Replying to every reply on your own post is the single highest-value action.**

**Links.** Readings conflict: "30–50% reach penalty, non-Premium link posts trending to zero engagement by March 2026" and "up to 94% visibility reduction in Q1 2026 tests" ([opentweet](https://opentweet.io/blog/x-algorithm-secrets-2026), [opentweet GitHub](https://opentweet.io/blog/x-algorithm-open-source-github-2026)) vs "no hard-coded link penalty anywhere in the open-source release" ([postory](https://postory.io/blog/x-algorithm-2026)). The safe play is the same: native video in the post, link in the first reply.

**Hashtags.** 1–2 niche tags fine; >2 trips the spam classifier (~−40%) ([opentweet](https://opentweet.io/blog/x-algorithm-open-source-github-2026)). The contest requires exactly two. Good.

**Video.** VQV only fires above a minimum duration (sub-threshold clips earn nothing on that signal); free accounts cap at 2 min 20 s ([opentweet](https://opentweet.io/blog/x-algorithm-open-source-github-2026), [buzzvoice](https://buzzvoice.com/blog/how-long-can-twitter-videos-be)). Watch behaviour: completion "falls off a cliff after 30 s"; 15–30 s is where most reposted clips sit; 30–90 s works for breakdowns with retention dropping steeply after 60 s; 70–85% of impressions are muted autoplay, so **burn in captions and make the first 5 seconds work silent**; vertical/square dominate ([buzzvoice](https://buzzvoice.com/blog/how-long-can-twitter-videos-be)). Postory recommends one 60 s+ video per week to unlock VQV ([postory](https://postory.io/blog/x-algorithm-2026)). **Resolution for this contest:** the 60 s master goes on LinkedIn; on X, post the 60 s master *and* pin a 20–30 s cut in a reply or quote 6–8 h later (quote-posting your own post after it has 10–15 replies "resets the clock" ([Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/how-to-go-viral-on-twitter-small-account/))).

**Long-form vs threads.** Single long posts (1,000–4,000 chars, Premium) get 40–60% more impressions than the same content as a thread, because each thread tweet is scored alone and the 2-minute dwell bonus accrues to one post ([moderncomment](https://moderncomment.com/blog/x-twitter-algorithm-changes-in-2026-what-creators-and-brands-need-to-know/), [tweetarchivist](https://www.tweetarchivist.com/twitter-algorithm-explained-2025)). Premium posts get a 1.3–1.6× boost in 2026 ([smmnut](https://smmnut.com/blog/does-x-premium-give-real-algorithm-advantages-2026/)). **[speculation]** Premium Basic (~₹200–250/month) is a legitimate buy here: it is not "paid promotion", it is platform membership, and it unlocks long posts and the boost. Check the contest's wording before buying.

**Cadence.** 3–5 standalone posts/day max; the diversity decay "halves your second post and crushes the third" in one viewer's session ([postory](https://postory.io/blog/x-algorithm-2026)).

**Realistic reach.** <1K followers: 100–500 impressions/post typical, 500–1,500 good, 1,500+ excellent; impression rate 0.5–2× followers typical, 5–10× excellent; median engagement rate 1.2% ([tweetarchivist](https://www.tweetarchivist.com/twitter-impressions-guide-2025), [xholic](https://xholic.ai/blog/twitter-engagement-rate-benchmarks-2026/)). Case: Sarvam's own 112-second voice-agents video from a 6-figure account got 1,479 likes / 53 replies ([link](https://x.com/SarvamAI/status/2087864104799314216)); a fan reshare of the same video got 22 likes ([link](https://x.com/_vmlops/status/2088193181846569327)).

**Timing (IST).** 9–11 AM and 6–9 PM IST; single peak Tue 10–11 AM; Mon–Thu; "very active 8–9 PM"; cricket match days spike; "English for professional content, Hindi reaches broader" ([opentweet India](https://opentweet.io/best-time-to-post/india), [Postpone India](https://www.postpone.app/tools/best-time-to-post/twitter/in/india), [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/best-time-to-post-on-twitter-x/)). India's AI/builder X crowd skews evening **[speculation]**; a 7:30–8:00 PM IST post with 90 minutes of reply time is a reasonable second slot.

**Creator payouts** are irrelevant (Revenue Sharing retired Aug 2026 for Original Content Rewards; required 500 verified followers + 5M impressions) ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/08/x-replaces-misaligned-revenue-sharing-program-with-original-content-rewards/), [X Help](https://help.x.com/en/using-x/creator-revenue-sharing)).

### 3.3 Cross-platform rules of thumb that both platforms reward

- First 3 seconds decide most of the reach of a video; a verbal hook plus a visual pattern interrupt lifts 3-second retention ~47%; hooks under 2 s complete 23% more than 4–5 s hooks; captions on hooks +32% completion (short-video industry data, not platform-specific) ([greenfroglabs](https://greenfroglabs.com/blog/anatomy-of-viral-hook), [virvid](https://virvid.ai/blog/ai-shorts-script-hook-ultimate-guide-2026)).
- Dean Seddon's 4,862 viral LinkedIn posts: hook in lines 1–3 (bold statement / provocative question / surprising stat), a relatable problem written to one person, scannable 2–3-sentence paragraphs, a 3–5-step framework, vivid analogy, vulnerability ("I got 3 likes—two were my mom"), clear CTA, status-quo challenge ([article](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/i-reviewed-4862-viral-linkedin-posts-here-what-found-dean-seddon-4ejce/)).
- "Usefulness is the new viral… the audience is tired of being impressed; they want to be helped"; raw AI-generated content stopped travelling in 2025 ([Kottapally](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/era-ai-slop-over-what-we-learned-from-linkedins-viral-kottapally-5ppgc/)).
- For hackathon sponsors specifically: "If I reward this project, will my company look good?" The winner's video becomes the sponsor's own marketing asset ([Ray Deck, five hackathon wins](https://sustained.substack.com/p/lessons-from-winning-five-hackathons)).

## 5. The 60-second demo video: blueprint

Constraints: Demo Day Drop judges "best 60-second demo video"; the same file is the nominated post's media on both platforms; Gnani forbids real PII or recorded calls (synthetic data only); judges and viewers mostly watch muted, on phones. Format: **9:16 vertical master, 1080×1920, burned-in captions (English, with Hindi/regional words in Devanagari/native script where spoken), ≤60.0 s**; export a 1:1 crop for X and a 20–30 s teaser cut.

| Time | Shot | On screen | Audio/caption | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:03 | **Cold open on the payoff.** A real person (your mother / a shopkeeper / a classmate) says one line in Hindi/Tamil/Hinglish on a phone, over visible street or kitchen noise; the transcript appears instantly and correctly | Big caption of the exact utterance; small label "Prisma v2.5 · live" | Natural speech, no music | 3-second rule; muted-autoplay-proof; "it understood that?!" is the hook ([buzzvoice](https://buzzvoice.com/blog/how-long-can-twitter-videos-be), [greenfroglabs](https://greenfroglabs.com/blog/anatomy-of-viral-hook)) |
| 0:03–0:08 | **Problem in one sentence with a number.** Cut to you, to camera, one line: "X crore people do Y by phone; today Z happens." | Caption | Your voice | Devpost/Reskilll: problem first, with a stat ([Reskilll](https://reskilll.com/blogs/hackathon-demo-presentation-tips-pitch-3-minutes-win-2026/)) |
| 0:08–0:12 | **Before.** Split screen: the same noisy clip through a generic ASR (garbled) | "Google/Whisper: ..." vs blank | — | Comparative numbers travel on Indian AI Twitter (§4 #8); Noise Canceller award |
| 0:12–0:40 | **The core action, uninterrupted.** The user does the one thing the app is for, end to end, in real time (no cuts that hide latency). Include one code-mix moment and one noisy moment. Show the Timbre voice replying. | Phone screen + the user's face in a corner; latency counter | Real dialogue; captions | Judges: "Does it work?" is #1; show, don't tell; no code editor, no terminal ([Reskilll](https://reskilll.com/blogs/hackathon-demo-presentation-tips-pitch-3-minutes-win-2026/), [Devpost](https://info.devpost.com/blog/6-tips-for-making-a-hackathon-demo-video)) |
| 0:40–0:46 | **The unexpected moment.** One beat the viewer can't predict: a dialect word handled, a mid-sentence language switch, a wrong turn the agent recovers from with humour, the user laughing | Reaction shot | — | GibberLink lesson; comedy drives comments (§4 #2, #10) |
| 0:46–0:53 | **Proof card.** WER on your synthetic noisy set vs baselines; latency; "Built on Prisma v2.5 + Timbre v2.5 (+ Evon v3.3)"; "₹0 infra · built in N days · 1 student" | Clean card, 3 numbers max | Voice-over | Gnani wants their models named; ₹/days jugaad line is press-liftable (§4 #4) |
| 0:53–0:58 | **Who it's for + what's next.** One sentence, real user's face | Caption | — | "Would real people use this?" |
| 0:58–1:00 | **End card:** project name, `#GnaniAI #GreatIndianAIInternshipChallenge`, your handle. No URL on screen (viewers can't click it; put the link in the comment) | — | — | Keeps the post link-free |

Production rules distilled from the guides: write the script first and read it aloud for time ([Devpost](https://info.devpost.com/blog/6-tips-for-making-a-hackathon-demo-video)); never speed up audio; record real microphone audio for *you*, but deliberately keep the *user's* audio raw so the noise is honest; don't open a code editor, don't demo half-built features, don't exceed the limit ("judges literally stop listening") ([Reskilll](https://reskilll.com/blogs/hackathon-demo-presentation-tips-pitch-3-minutes-win-2026/)); production polish does not score, a working demo does ([hackathon.com tips](https://tips.hackathon.com/article/creating-the-best-demo-video-for-a-hackathon-what-to-know)); upload natively (not a YouTube link) on both platforms; also upload to YouTube unlisted/public as the submission's permanent copy ([Devpost](https://info.devpost.com/blog/6-tips-for-making-a-hackathon-demo-video)). Reserve a full day for recording and cutting; "video uploads can take hours" ([Devpost WLH tips](https://worldslargesthackathon.devpost.com/updates/36832-demo-video-tips)).

Caption text for the nominated LinkedIn post (structure, not copy): line 1 the user's quote or the ₹/days number; line 2 the problem to one person; 3–6 short lines on what it does and the three proof numbers; one honest line on what broke; the model names; a *genuine* question that invites a specific answer (not "agree?"); the two hashtags; tag `gnani.ai` + ≤2 people. 600–1,000 characters; no link; ≤5 emojis; no blank-line padding ([AuthoredUp](https://authoredup.com/blog/linkedin-algorithm), [stan.store](https://stan.store/blog/linkedin-algorithm/)). X version: the video, 2–4 lines, the two hashtags, `@GnaniAi`; link, repo and long write-up in replies (or one long-form post if Premium).

## 6. Legitimate distribution playbook for a solo student with a small following

Everything here is allowed under the rules (no purchase, bots, paid promotion, or like-for-like exchanges). Asking real people who know you to *watch and say what they think* is organic; coordinating reciprocal engagement is not.

### 6.1 Pre-launch (now → Oct 19): build the surfaces that will carry the post
1. **Profile = landing page.** Headline names the problem and the model ("Building Hinglish voice agents on Gnani Prisma/Timbre · NSUT"); Featured section holds the repo and a teaser; 10–15 consistent keywords across headline/about/posts so the interest graph classifies you ([stan.store](https://stan.store/blog/linkedin-algorithm/)). Same on X: bio with a specific promise; pinned teaser ([Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/how-to-go-viral-on-twitter-small-account/)).
2. **Warm the graph with comments, not posts.** 10–15 substantive (2–3 sentence) comments/day on posts by Gnani staff (Ganesh, Om Joshi, Thoshith S, Vasuta Agarwal), Indian voice-AI people, NSUT alumni, and Indic-AI researchers; on X, 5–10 substantive replies/day to 5K–50K-follower accounts within 15 minutes of their posting ([hyperclapper](https://www.hyperclapper.com/blog-posts/linkedin-organic-reach-decline-2026-algorithm-penalty), [Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/how-to-go-viral-on-twitter-small-account/)). Expected: 150–400 new X followers in three weeks; more importantly, a handful of people who will recognise your name in their notifications.
3. **Build in public, 2–3 posts/week, never two in 24 h:** the noisy-audio test set, a WER chart vs baselines, a failure clip, a "what my mother said when it understood her" story. Each is a small reach test; keep what works. These are *not* the nominated posts ([stan.store](https://stan.store/blog/linkedin-algorithm/)).
4. **Assemble a 30-person "first hour" list** of real people who know you (classmates, seniors, NSUT clubs, hackathon teammates, professors, family on LinkedIn). You will message them *once*, on launch morning, with the link and no instruction beyond "would love your honest reaction". Their comments must be their own words (≥5 words; "Great post!" is now worth nothing) ([SocialPilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/blog/linkedin-algorithm)).
5. **Line up two credible commenters** for the first hour: a professor or an industry alum who can write one sentence about why the noisy-audio result matters. Institutional validation is the highest-engagement content type for Indian AI pages (§4 #6).
6. **Gnani Discord.** Be visibly helpful there now (answer other participants' API questions). Post your teaser there on launch day; staff and 480 peers are the most relevant audience that exists ([Discord](https://discord.gg/aECcvtABpq)).
7. **Decide the X Premium question** (see §3.2). **[speculation]** Likely worth it for the 1.3–1.6× boost and long-form post; confirm with Gnani (Discord/email) that platform membership is not "paid promotion".

### 6.2 Launch day (target **Wed Oct 21 or Tue Oct 27, 08:00 IST** for LinkedIn; X the same evening 19:30 IST)
Why Oct 20–27: engagement accumulates until the cutoff; a post needs ~2 weeks of life plus a buffer for a second attempt if it dies in hour one; Nov 10 is the hard close. Only the first nominated post per platform counts, so launch once, well.

| Time (IST) | Action |
|---|---|

- **LinkedIn: 3 posts/week, Tue/Wed/Thu 08:00 IST**, each a *new angle* on the same build (a user story, a benchmark, a failure + fix, a regional-language variant, a "what Gnani's model got right that X didn't"). If a Reel pops, screenshot its numbers into a LinkedIn comment on the nominated post.

- **College amplification that is legitimate:** ask the NSUT placement cell / department / tech societies to *feature* the project (their pages posting about a student is editorial, not an exchange); ask a professor to comment; present at a club meeting and ask attendees to watch the video live from their phones (real views, real reactions).
- **Tag collaborators who actually contributed**: a voice-donor family member who is on LinkedIn, a teammate, a mentor. Not Gnani staff en masse.
- **Watch the hashtag daily** (LinkedIn search for `#GreatIndianAIInternshipChallenge`, X search). Comment substantively on other entrants' posts; the community is small (482 on Discord) and reciprocity *of attention* (not of likes) is how you become the entrant everyone has seen. Don't propose or join any "support each other" like groups: that is the banned "engagement exchange".
- **Nov 8–9:** last LinkedIn angle post and X recap; do not change the nominated posts. **Nov 10:** submit by midday IST; confirm the cutoff time in Discord earlier (open question in `01-hackathon-overview.md`).

### 6.4 What "good" looks like for this account
Given §3 benchmarks (≤1K followers: median ~200 LinkedIn impressions, top-decile ~1,300; X 100–500 impressions typical), realistic targets with the above playbook **[speculation]**: LinkedIn nominated post 3–10K impressions, 150–400 reactions, 40–120 comments; X 10–50K impressions, 100–500 likes, 30–100 replies. A breakout (a reshare by Ganesh or a national-press pickup of the clip) is the only path to 1K+ reactions, and the §5 blueprint is built to make that reshare easy.

## 7. Implications for idea choice

### 7.1 What is inherently shareable to an Indian audience (evidence from §4)
1. **A real elder/family member speaking their own language and being understood.** The three largest clips (2M+ views; 228K likes; Sophie) are all this. Highest emotive load; also the cleanest demonstration of Prisma's code-mix/dialect claims. Risk: ridicule backlash; mitigate by consent, making the elder the hero, and having them *benefit* on camera.
2. **Before/after on noisy phone audio with a competitor side-by-side.** Indian AI X runs on comparative numbers and national-pride arcs (Sarvam-M saga, 1,630-like retrospective). Prisma's own Kathbath-Noisy numbers (6.2% vs Google 12.9%, Whisper 22.1%) are the template. Visually strong (waveform + two transcripts). Also wins the Noise Canceller frame.
3. **Jugaad number in the headline.** "₹25,000", "built in 9 days", "one student, one phone". Press lifts numbers verbatim.
4. **Regional pride / non-Hindi language.** Multiplies audiences (Tamil/Bengali/Kannada communities reshare their own); matches the Main Character award. Sophie (UP), Hacker House (Bengaluru) both carried a place.
5. **A person reacting (laughing, surprised) in frame.** Classmates reacting to Sophie; hacker-house faces. Reaction is the shareable unit.
6. **Humour from a misunderstanding → recovery.** Dadi's "it must wander the streets". Drives comments, which drive reach.
7. **Institutional/third-party validation in the first hour.** Sarvam's top LinkedIn post is PM Modi; Gnani's top posts are funding/GFF. For a student: a professor or an alum's comment, or Gnani staff's reshare.
8. **Audible novelty.** GibberLink's chirp. For voice AI: a seamless mid-sentence Hinglish switch, a cloned voice, a dialect word.

Inherently *un*-shareable (the floor in §4 #12): pipelines, dashboards, "STT→LLM→TTS", architecture diagrams, "Excited to share", enterprise-only use cases with no person on screen, anything that needs a paragraph to explain why it matters.

### 7.3 Shareability rubric (apply to every candidate idea; 0–3 each, max 21)
| Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Human in frame** | none | narrator only | one user acting | a real user *reacting* (laugh/surprise) |
| **Hearable language moment** | English only | Hindi only | code-mix handled | dialect/regional + code-mix handled, visibly transcribed |
| **Visible before/after** | none | claimed in text | shown once | split-screen live vs baseline with a number |
| **Headline number** | none | a vague claim | one metric | ₹/days/WER that a journalist could paste |
| **Unexpected moment** | none | mild | one surprise | one surprise + one laugh |
| **Relatability of problem** (one sentence, one person) | needs a paragraph | niche B2B | common Indian household/work task | universal and emotional (family, money, health) |
| **Sponsor-reshare appeal** (does Gnani look good?) | wrapper-like | model named in text | model name + metric on screen | demonstrates a Gnani-specific claim (noisy 8 kHz, code-mix) competitors fail |

Thresholds **[speculation]**: ≥17 = can plausibly earn a Gnani reshare and a few hundred reactions; 14–16 = solid; <14 = redesign the demo before building more.

Scored quickly: idea #1 above ≈ 19–20; #2 ≈ 14–15 (add a reacting user to lift it); #3 ≈ 15–16; #7 ≈ 5–7.

### 7.4 Two cross-cutting design rules from the evidence
- **Build the demo for the clip, then build the product behind it.** Decide the 0:00–0:03 shot first (whose voice, which sentence, which noise), because that shot is most of the reach and most of the "Demo Day Drop" score.
- **Make the technical claim falsifiable and put it on screen.** Indian AI Twitter's biggest 2025–26 story was a VC counting downloads; the fastest way to earn its respect is to show a number against a named baseline and invite replication (repo + synthetic test set in the first comment). That is also what the interview will probe.
